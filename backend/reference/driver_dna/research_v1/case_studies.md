# Deliverable F — Driver comparison case studies

Research cut-off 2026-11-12. Evidence IDs refer to `driver_evidence.csv`. The sequences and charts linked below belong to their original publishers. A press quote records what its speaker reported. A qualifying result identifies a lap or session. Both can guide a hypothesis about technique. Validation requires repeated comparable segments and the channels that would measure the hypothesis.

## 1. Max Verstappen and Lewis Hamilton

**Balance and steering.** Alex Albon described Verstappen’s appetite for a very responsive front end in their 2019–20 Red Bull partnership (EV-VER001). Verstappen also described compensating for lazy turn-in early in 2022 as Red Bull reduced weight and changed its distribution (EV-VER002). Mark Hughes reported that Hamilton could work with some rear instability on entry, including in faster turns (EV-HAM003). Chandhok and Kalinauckas observed restrained hand movement from Verstappen in selected 2023 qualifying footage (EV-VER005). Steering input and the car’s response to it need their own fields. The sources suggest overlapping tolerance for a responsive entry but provide no comparable steering-rate measure. [Albon’s account](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/its-eye-watering-albon-shares-the-secrets-of-verstappens-unique-driving.3Dlce2QkRGzUDLMtDf3dOs), [Verstappen’s account](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/verstappen-dispels-popular-driving-style-myth-after-first-laps-in-red-bulls.3Iu22u4oZbq0QpZo6sKYS2), [Hamilton technical retrospective](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/long-read-the-constant-evolution-behind-hamiltons-greatness.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o), [2023 onboard study](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-driving-style-secrets-of-f1s-current-stars/10568984/).

**Heavy braking and fast sequences.** Hughes and Jolyon Palmer describe hard initial braking and entry-speed retention for Hamilton (EV-HAM001, EV-HAM002). At Jeddah in 2021 Verstappen was ahead in the first two qualifying sectors before his last-corner lock-up. The official split-screen is a specific fast-sequence and heavy-stop example. Zandvoort’s banked Turn 2–3 sequence supplies a second comparison, but the Red Bull and Mercedes differed in wheelbase and how the rear responded over the banking. [Jeddah split-screen and analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/exclusive-verstappen-and-hamiltons-decisive-saudi-arabian-gp-qualifying-laps.5dh3fX6T0SQJp5KpkEEvqN), [Zandvoort technical analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-tuesday-why-zandvoorts-banking-left-mercedes-playing-catch-up-to-red.6Cj5UjKvzcn1rjRJzOM0RY).

**Comparison design.** Use completed laps from both drivers to compare binary brake onset, pre-apex speed loss, minimum speed and throttle recovery by corner type. Keep the aborted Jeddah lap available for event annotation with its incomplete final segment excluded from the reference. Separate tow, wing specification, power unit mode and tyre state wherever verified. Current evidence supports context-specific braking hypotheses and qualitative balance resemblance. Brake pressure, steering rate and a driver-only difference in low-speed technique remain open.

## 2. Oscar Piastri and Lando Norris

**Same team, different feel.** Norris says he tends to brake late (EV-NOR001). Palmer’s examples include overtakes (EV-NOR002). Hughes and Piola describe more combined braking and cornering from Norris than from Piastri (EV-NOR003, EV-PIA002). Piastri tried an alternative front suspension at Montréal and stayed with the standard MCL39 layout for continuity (EV-PIA003). Norris reported diminished feedback relative to the prior year (EV-NOR004). Piastri’s earlier self-description as precise and consistent reflects junior racing (EV-PIA001). These accounts support a relative entry-overlap hypothesis. They leave exact release shape, steering smoothness and the preferred rear balance of either driver unmeasured. [Norris interview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/f1-explains-lando-norris-on-driving-styles-champagne-slams-and-special.u2anwNmnDl5vgj34QpRyo), [Racing ID synopsis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/watch-how-norris-and-perez-have-gained-and-lost-from-their-braking-styles.7w2bCBaqBDlHhe0RUVMlbi.7w2bCBaqBDlHhe0RUVMlbi), [2025 suspension and corner-entry analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-weekly-the-unique-mclaren-design-feature-that-seems-to-have-affected.6Nu1IcMy8bYP6CoaaAEK3G), [Follow-up analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-weekly-the-ingenious-changes-mclaren-have-made-to-help-norris-re-find.3WYpLDUx9pBNKUvgHKrfhA), [Piastri’s junior-era description](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/why-piastri-is-using-hamilton-and-norris-as-inspiration-ahead-of-his-f1.7ai0IswDyj9HaOYOUSbKjM).

**Fast and slow corners.** Chandhok and Kalinauckas saw less abrupt hand movement from Piastri than Norris in selected fast corners in 2023 (EV-PIA005). Silverstone 2025 supplies a separate wet-race observation for Piastri (EV-PIA004). Low-speed throttle recovery remains unmeasured. Select paired dry qualifying laps before and after the suspension split and stratify by tight stops, sustained high-speed corners and medium-speed entries. The change in front geometry is a useful comparison breakpoint. Setup, tyres and traffic remain potential confounders even before the split. [2023 onboard study](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-driving-style-secrets-of-f1s-current-stars/10568984/), [Silverstone team report](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-race-day-in-great-britain-2025.5KJQegr9vYA4g9pCrLUec).

**Comparison design.** Build paired distributions for brake-switch onset and duration, speed minimum and throttle rise. Public channels cannot resolve pressure release or direct steering/braking overlap. A brake-plus-geometric-turn proxy, if supported by adequate position sampling, needs a separate feature ID and validation against known steering traces. A game player can measure their own overlap directly at the normalized command level and see the professional hypothesis as a sourced qualitative observation.

## 3. Oscar Piastri and George Russell

**Comparable session candidate.** Both produced completed Q3 laps at Sakhir in 2025, where Piastri took pole and Russell was second-fastest in qualifying. Sakhir’s heavy stops and its fast section allow the same test to include braking onset, minimum-speed strategy and throttle timing in multiple corner classes. Their McLaren and Mercedes packages and setup choices remain confounders. Qualifying positions carry no direct technique inference. [Bahrain qualifying report](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/piastri-beats-russell-and-leclerc-to-pole-position-during-bahrain-grand-prix.4yw7fVrWBvxYZunZUU0KuX).

**What the sources establish.** The MCL39 analysis supports a relative entry observation for Piastri against Norris (EV-PIA002). Selected 2023 onboard footage gives a qualitative account of Russell’s decisive initial steering input (EV-RUS004). At Williams, engineer Dave Robson recalled Russell as more tolerant than Albon during initial turn-in and more sensitive later in the corner. Robson specifically raised the effect of different cars (EV-RUS003). Russell’s brake-by-wire problem at Sakhir illustrates adaptation to an abnormal braking response, and identifies laps to exclude from a regular-technique baseline (EV-RUS001). No common reference driver or calibrated scale makes the two teammate comparisons transitive. [McLaren entry analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-weekly-the-unique-mclaren-design-feature-that-seems-to-have-affected.6Nu1IcMy8bYP6CoaaAEK3G), [2023 onboard study](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-driving-style-secrets-of-f1s-current-stars/10568984/), [Williams engineer interview](https://www.the-race.com/formula-1/thriving-albon-has-helped-williams-kick-its-russell-hangover/), [Mercedes race report](https://www.mercedesamgf1.com/reports/race-report-bahrain-grand-prix-third-podium-four-races-george-russell).

**Comparison design.** Use dry qualifying and stable practice laps at the same session stage, with position and timing checks on Turns 1 and 10 and fast sections such as Turns 5–7. Compare timing of deceleration and throttle restoration separately. Current evidence does not resolve which driver overlaps steering and braking more, their normal release curves, preferred balance or who commits earlier in fast corners.

## 4. Max Verstappen and Charles Leclerc

**Balance.** Leclerc explicitly described preferring oversteer while limiting it at Paul Ricard to protect the rears (EV-LEC001, EV-LEC003). Albon’s account of Verstappen emphasizes immediate front response (EV-VER001). Front response at turn-in and an oversteering car state are different variables and may coexist to different degrees across a corner. Leclerc’s complaints about unpredictable snap oversteer in Spain in 2023 are compatible with his earlier stated preference. [Paul Ricard debrief](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/monday-morning-debrief-how-leclercs-crash-caused-a-domino-effect-that-shaped.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV), [Albon on Verstappen](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/its-eye-watering-albon-shares-the-secrets-of-verstappens-unique-driving.3Dlce2QkRGzUDLMtDf3dOs), [Spanish qualifying driver quotes](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-qualifying-in-spain-2023.1oP37p28PdxFUTAHmHB963).

**Corner evidence.** Formula 1’s Imola analysis ties Leclerc’s higher speed and earlier kerb approach to a specific Variante Alta incident (EV-LEC004). Verstappen’s RB16B could place the car for the next banked corner at Zandvoort in 2021 (EV-VER004). Separate events and machinery prevent inferring a normal risk or steering difference. At Zandvoort in 2022 both set completed, closely timed Q3 laps, giving a candidate paired comparison in the new ground-effect era. Their 2022 Singapore comparison has an aborted Verstappen lap in changing conditions and serves mainly as a sample-selection example. [Imola data analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/data-deep-dive-how-leclerc-ended-up-losing-out-on-p2-and-falling-further-at.5XGqQ7r8Gp4pYjdlvfyGYV), [Zandvoort 2021 analysis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/tech-tuesday-why-zandvoorts-banking-left-mercedes-playing-catch-up-to-red.6Cj5UjKvzcn1rjRJzOM0RY), [Zandvoort 2022 qualifying](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/verstappen-beats-leclerc-to-zandvoort-pole-by-0-021s.2QpeEgbq9VbLr2lbK2jFI5), [Singapore comparison](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/watch-leclerc-and-verstappens-final-singapore-qualifying-laps-compared.5KGr70nBfKA6VDhU06LoqT).

**Comparison design.** For completed laps compare heavy-stop brake onset and exit throttle separately from high-speed lift and speed retention. Banked corners need their own tag. Pressure, steering and steady-state balance still require separate evidence. Red Bull and Ferrari aero and traction differences can produce a speed-trace difference with identical driver intent.

## 5. Fernando Alonso and Lewis Hamilton

**Steering and braking.** Alonso described using a pronounced steering input to sense front-tyre response and working with Alpine on power steering, brake balance and engine braking in 2021 (EV-ALO001, EV-ALO002). Symonds recalled a rapid, late steering input during the Renault years and specifically cautioned that the later visible pattern differed (EV-ALO003). These accounts are both phase- and era-specific. Hamilton’s technical retrospective emphasizes brake force and modulation alongside tolerance for some rear movement on entry (EV-HAM001, EV-HAM003). No matched telemetry establishes which driver has faster steering onset, later braking, or more front-axle loading. [Alonso interview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/alonso-explains-key-change-alpine-made-to-the-car-to-suit-his-driving-style.1c5t3PKIHbRSidjPkJbYTt.1c5t3PKIHbRSidjPkJbYTt), [Symonds retrospective](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/march-2017/28/pat-symonds-senna-schumacher-alonso/), [Hamilton technical retrospective](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/long-read-the-constant-evolution-behind-hamiltons-greatness.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o).

**Racecraft example.** At the Hungaroring in 2021 Alonso repeatedly positioned the car to defend against Hamilton through Turns 2 and 4 before a lock-up opened the pass (EV-ALO004). This tests traffic-aware position and response to an opponent over a slow-to-fast sequence. The defending and attacking tasks, car and tyres differed. The relevant teammate control for peak-lap technique would be archived 2007 McLaren telemetry with task, setup and tyres recorded. Public 2007 race times alone leave those channels unobserved. [Hungarian GP account](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/hamilton-hails-really-great-battle-with-alonso-but-spaniard-says-old-rival.FBgAtjBbkziJat8PLla3o), [2007 results archive](https://www.formula1.com/en/results/2007/races).

**Comparison design.** Use modern overlapping seasons to test repeatability of brake and speed proxies across matched sessions. Preserve a racecraft label on the Hungaroring example. Evidence for steering-wheel feedback or input shape belongs to qualitative evidence until relevant raw controls can be accessed. The evidence set supplies no independent low-speed versus high-speed style ranking.

## 6. Charles Leclerc and Carlos Sainz

**Same chassis and different tolerances.** Leclerc explicitly favoured some oversteer in 2022 but avoided leaning on that balance at Paul Ricard (EV-LEC001). Sainz said that the early F1-75 was too pointy for his taste and required unfamiliar techniques (EV-SAI001, EV-SAI002). He later reported a broader adaptation range after that season (EV-SAI003). The evidence supports a contextual difference in comfort with the early F1-75’s response. A separate 2023 onboard study described a two-stage entry from both, with more gradual steering from Sainz in selected laps (EV-LEC006, EV-SAI006). It supplies no numeric understeer preference or steering-rate ordering. [Paul Ricard debrief](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/monday-morning-debrief-how-leclercs-crash-caused-a-domino-effect-that-shaped.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV), [Sainz on balance](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/why-smart-capable-sainz-can-still-be-a-factor-in-the-2022-title-fight.22F1Sj2iX6EmbUOBqhry0o), [Sainz on adaptation](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/im-hustling-out-there-sainz-admits-hes-using-techniques-im-not-used-to-to.hlpxtdrh9ydJldxpz19yQ), [2023 onboard study](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/the-driving-style-secrets-of-f1s-current-stars/10568984/), [2023 follow-up](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/sainz-says-he-feels-much-more-prepared-heading-into-2023-as-leclerc.1Z9HETnDBnaGyrnFfVntTt).

**Braking and corner speed.** Racing ID highlights differences in downshift use (EV-SAI005, EV-LEC005). The synopsis leaves the direction and timing unreported. At Paul Ricard Sainz appeared comfortable through long, fast bends (EV-SAI004). His Q3 tow role removes a straightforward final-lap comparison. At the 2023 Spanish qualifying session, Leclerc reported a rear-grip problem (EV-LEC002), creating another reason to reject a naïve ranking. The sources leave normal slow-corner entry speed and throttle progression unresolved. [Racing ID synopsis](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/watch-how-norris-and-perez-have-gained-and-lost-from-their-braking-styles.7w2bCBaqBDlHhe0RUVMlbi.7w2bCBaqBDlHhe0RUVMlbi), [Paul Ricard debrief](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/monday-morning-debrief-how-leclercs-crash-caused-a-domino-effect-that-shaped.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV.1SpXv2Tjm1YuWIiuIW8yCV), [2023 Spanish qualifying quotes](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/what-the-teams-said-qualifying-in-spain-2023.1oP37p28PdxFUTAHmHB963).

**Comparison design.** Pair valid Ferrari laps from the same session and segment, with evidence on tow, traffic and specification where available. Compare gear transitions and brake-state timing in slow stops and throttle recovery in long versus short exits. Use Paul Ricard for a historical hypothesis and verify on complete laps from independent sessions. Use the contextual balance quotes for explanation.

## 7. Sebastian Vettel and Lewis Hamilton

**Era-specific technique.** Vettel’s race engineer Guillaume Rocquelin identified stable entry and exhaust-assisted rear load as important to the 2010 Red Bull package. Throttle changes could alter diffuser load as well as drive torque (EV-VET001, EV-VET002). Adrian Newey reported that the loss of the blown diffuser in 2014 required adjustments for Vettel (EV-VET003). Hughes described Hamilton carrying entry speed with strong braking and tolerating some rear movement (EV-HAM001, EV-HAM003). Together the sources motivate testing how each balances speed loss and rotation, but conditions, control maps and rear aerodynamic response differ. [Rocquelin interview](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/articles/single-seaters/f1/vettels-first-f1-title-how-driver-and-car-achieved-perfect-harmony-in-2010/), [Newey interview](https://adamcooperf1.com/2014/06/03/adrian-newey-seb-has-a-very-particular-way-of-driving/), [Hamilton technical retrospective](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/long-read-the-constant-evolution-behind-hamiltons-greatness.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o.6CptiTarBKWmhjKBVCPZ5o).

**Comparison design.** Use overlapping Ferrari–Mercedes qualifying seasons such as 2018–19 as candidate sources for speed and brake-state alignment, where archive completeness permits. Treat the 2010 and 2014 Red Bull material as context and use a separate regulation-era key for each. A throttle percentage or onset observed in the game does not recreate the diffuser mechanism. The current sources do not establish a recurring high-speed or low-speed advantage for either driver or a comparable steering trace.

## 8. Michael Schumacher and other historic drivers

**Schumacher and Senna.** Symonds recalled Schumacher exploiting a lively Benetton setup and Barnard described his preference for an immediate front response and stronger engine braking during the early Ferrari tests (EV-MSC001, EV-MSC002). McLaren’s Steve Nichols recalled Senna’s repeated throttle applications and offered possible turbo-response and grip-probing explanations (EV-SEN001). Honda’s Takeo Kiuchi described a role for throttle and yaw in 1990–92, including naturally aspirated machinery (EV-SEN002). The accounts support separate rotation hypotheses. They establish neither the exact control schedules nor a single explanation across the cars Senna drove. [Symonds interview](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/march-2017/28/pat-symonds-senna-schumacher-alonso/), [Barnard interview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/why-did-schumacher-struggle-at-mercedes-tech-guru-john-barnard-shares-his.uE8kSDNHuk2kbCs5hAmS2.uE8kSDNHuk2kbCs5hAmS2), [McLaren account](https://www.mclaren.com/racing/heritage/formula-1/drivers/ayrton-senna/how-good-actually-was-ayrton-senna-hint-exceptionally/), [Kiuchi interview](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/archive/article/may-2004/34/light-end-tunnel/).

**Schumacher and Prost.** Prost described a brake- and equipment-preserving approach and recalled preferring to forego power steering and ABS in the 1993 Williams because he wanted different feedback (EV-PRO001, EV-PRO002). The control systems and purpose differ from Schumacher’s Ferrari test, and neither source quantifies release slope. Jim Clark also described overlapping braking and turning in the Lotus, illustrating how a broad technique category can recur in different eras with different mechanics (EV-CLA001). [Prost on conserving the car](https://www.motorsportmagazine.com/articles/single-seaters/f1/the-reason-for-alain-prosts-smooth-driving-style-i-couldnt-afford-a-mechanic/), [Prost transcript](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/full-transcript-read-every-word-from-four-time-world-champion-alain-prosts.24IUj5h536Ih9IwT09FgjG), [Clark period interview](https://www.autosport.com/f1/news/classic-interview-jim-clark-and-graham-hill-5102381/5102381/).

**Schumacher and Hamilton.** Shovlin recalled that both could work with the balance available and layer several instructions into a lap (EV-MSC003, EV-HAM006). This qualifies the applicability of Schumacher’s responsive-front preference outside the Benetton/Ferrari contexts. Separate test car, race setup and control era when describing balance. Sparse archival sources leave high-speed versus low-speed differences and detailed control comparisons unknown. The historical accounts are suitable for sourced references in coaching explanations when a specific observed technique resembles one of them. [Shovlin interview](https://www.formula1.com/en/latest/article/schumacher-and-hamilton-more-similar-than-you-might-believe-says-mercedes.6ZEBuSYYReMF8xtOXzC4Ve).

## How to compare traces

This schematic shows feature alignment and does not depict a driver or a sampled trace. Align both traces to the same verified reference geometry and direction. Plot observed points and gaps, or link to publisher visuals when redistribution rights are unavailable. Keep onboards as qualitative event evidence unless the measurement method validates the indicator, timestamp and distance axes. Report repeated-corner and repeated-event sample sizes separately, preserve corner class and tyre conditions, and retain the uncertainty and abstention status in the comparison result.
